01 / About me

About my research journey

I study binocular vision and stereo depth reconstruction: how a model can establish correspondences between left and right images, estimate disparity, and recover the three-dimensional structure of a scene.

My current interests center on the conditions that make correspondence unreliable—especially weak illumination, rain, fog, reduced contrast, and ambiguous visual features. I am interested in understanding these failure modes through controlled evaluation and exploring approaches that improve robustness without sacrificing computational efficiency.

As an early-stage researcher, I aim to connect fundamental stereo matching problems with practical depth perception systems that can work beyond ideal imaging conditions.

02 / Research interests

Research interests

Exploring dependable stereo geometry, visual correspondence, and efficient perception in real-world environments.

01

Stereo Matching & Depth Reconstruction

Investigating binocular correspondence, disparity estimation, and the recovery of dense scene depth from synchronized image pairs.

02

Low-Light & Adverse-Weather Robustness

Understanding how poor illumination, rain, fog, reduced contrast, and image noise affect stereo features and matching reliability.

03

Reliable Matching & Evaluation

Studying correspondence ambiguity, matching failures, and experimental evaluation across diverse real-world imaging conditions.

04

Lightweight & Deployable Stereo Vision

Exploring efficient depth-estimation networks that balance accuracy, robustness, inference speed, and resource constraints.
